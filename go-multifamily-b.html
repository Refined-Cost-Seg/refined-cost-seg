What you actually receive

A cost segregation study reallocates the components of your property into shorter depreciation lives — 5, 15, and 27.5 or 39 year — instead of depreciating the whole building on one long schedule. The total you can claim over the life of the property doesn't change; the timing does.

Every allocation is traced to its cost basis and checked against the IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide before delivery. Questions we get

My building is held in an LLC or partnership — does that change anything?

It changes your deadline more than it changes the study. Partnership returns are generally due March 15, or September 15 on extension, so the useful window for getting a study done sits earlier in the year than most people assume. The study itself is the same document; how the deductions flow through to you is a conversation for your CPA.

Do I need to have bought the property recently?

No. Studies are most often done in the year of purchase or after a renovation, but a property you've owned for several years can still be studied. If the property was placed in service in a prior year, catching up the depreciation is handled by your CPA on Form 3115 — we'll explain what they need.

Will this create a problem if I'm audited?

A study should stand on its documentation. Ours shows the component detail and the cost basis behind every line, so the allocations can be traced rather than asserted. The method follows the IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide, every study passes quality-control review before delivery, and Audit Support is available as an option if you'd like representation included.

Does my CPA have to be involved?

You don't need their permission to get an estimate. You will want them involved before you file — the study is a document they use, not a filing we make. Many of our clients loop their CPA in once they see the estimate, and we're happy to talk to them directly.

How long does it take?

Once we have your questionnaire and documents, a typical residential file moves through production and quality-control review in a matter of days rather than months. If you're working against a filing deadline, tell us in the questionnaire and we'll be straight with you about whether we can make it.

What if the numbers don't justify it?

Then we'll say so. The estimate exists precisely so you can decide before spending anything. Smaller or lower-basis properties sometimes don't move enough to be worth the exercise, and we'd rather tell you that than sell you a study.
