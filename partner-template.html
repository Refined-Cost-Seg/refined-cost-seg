Why it matters

Turn depreciation you'd wait decades for into first-year cash flow.

A residential building normally depreciates over 27.5 years. A cost segregation study identifies the parts of your property that legitimately qualify for faster 5-, 7-, and 15-year schedules — flooring, appliances, specialty electrical and plumbing, landscaping, and more — so those deductions come forward instead of trickling in over decades.

The result is a larger up-front deduction on the return your preparer is working on, and more cash working for you now instead of decades from now.

Common questions

Good questions, answered.

My preparer already depreciates the property. Isn't this the same thing?

No. Standard depreciation puts nearly the whole building on one long schedule. A study breaks the property into its actual components and documents which ones qualify for shorter recovery periods. Your preparer then implements those numbers on the return.

Is this legitimate and audit-ready?

Yes. Every study follows the IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide and passes a rigorous quality-control review before delivery, with documented basis and cost sources for substantiation.

Do I need a site visit or appointment?

No. Everything is handled remotely from a short online intake, your closing statement, and a few photos.

Can I actually use the deduction this year?

Often, but not always — rental losses run into the passive-activity rules, and how much you can use depends on your participation and your overall tax picture. That call belongs with your tax preparer, and it's worth asking before you order.

When do I see the benefit?

Your deduction generally applies on the return for the year the property was placed in service — the day it was ready and available for guests or tenants. Your preparer will confirm the timing for your situation.
