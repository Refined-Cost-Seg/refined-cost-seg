Buying a short-term rental in December: can you still meet material participation this year?

Refined Cost SegregationResidential cost segregation

Short answer: usually yes. Close on a short-term rental in early December, get it listed and available before year end, and you can still be a material participant for that tax year. The 500-hour test is out of reach in three weeks. But there are seven material participation tests, and two of the other six do not care how few hours you put in — only how your hours compare to everyone else's. In a three-week activity, nobody has many hours.

That is the part most people get backwards. A late closing does not shrink your chance of qualifying. It shrinks the denominator.

Why a December short-term rental is a different animal than a December long-term rental

A long-term rental is a rental activity, full stop: its losses are passive unless you qualify as a real estate professional, a bar most W-2 earners will not clear.

A short-term rental is not automatically a rental activity at all. The regulations carve an activity out of the rental definition when the average period of customer use is seven days or less, treating it as a trade or business instead, and from there the losses are non-passive if — and only if — you materially participate. No real estate professional status required. Check how the seven-day average is calculated before relying on it: the average is of all stays, not the typical one. So a December acquisition has two independent gates, both measured for the tax year.

Which material participation test can you actually meet in three weeks?

Of the seven tests, three matter here. The 500-hour test you can forget: three weeks of part-time work on one property is not 500 hours, and nobody should be writing down numbers that say otherwise.

The substantially-all test. No hour floor whatsoever. You qualify if your participation constitutes substantially all of the participation by every individual in the activity that year. Handle the furnishing, listing, photos, pricing, messaging and turnovers yourself, with nobody else doing meaningful work, and you meet this test on twenty hours. Or ten.

The 100-hour test. More than 100 hours, and no other individual spends more time than you. Harder in three weeks, but reachable if you are doing the setup yourself — a furnishing push burns hours fast.

Note which direction the short window pushes you: the substantially-all test gets easier the shorter the year, because there is less total participation by anyone to be substantially all of. Investor-capacity hours — reviewing statements, studying the market — count for nobody, yours or theirs. And it is a year-by-year test: qualifying for 2026 says nothing about 2027, when twelve months of cleaner and co-host hours stack against you. Our piece on material participation for short-term rentals covers the records that make this defensible.

What a December placed-in-service date actually requires

Placed in service means ready and available for its intended use: furnished, cleaned, licensed where a license is required, and live on a booking platform at a bookable price. It does not mean booked. A property listed December 12 whose first guest arrives January 3 is still placed in service in December, and that is where depreciation starts. We cover the documentation that proves it in our piece on December closings. What costs you the year is a property that closed December 1 and sat half-furnished until February — no listing, no availability, no 2026 deduction.

The math on a $640,000 short-term rental listed December 12

A cabin bought for $640,000, contract signed November 2026, closing December 1, listed December 12. Land is $96,000, so depreciable basis is $544,000, and you spent $28,000 furnishing it, placed in service the same day.

A short-term rental study typically reclassifies around 30% of basis into 5-, 7- and 15-year property — appliances, cabinetry, flooring, decorative lighting, specialty electrical, land improvements. That is roughly $163,200 out of the 27.5-year bucket, plus the $28,000 of furniture, which is 5-year property on its own: $191,200 of short-life property.

Because the property was acquired (contract signed) after January 19, 2025, that short-life property gets 100% bonus depreciation, so the whole $191,200 is deductible in 2026. At a 32% marginal federal rate, that is about $61,100 in first-year federal tax savings, plus whatever your state allows. The remaining building basis contributes almost nothing in a two-week December.

But that $61,100 only lands against your W-2 income if the loss is non-passive. Fail material participation and the deduction does not vanish — it suspends, waiting for passive income or a sale. Same numbers, very different cash flow. We also broke down furniture and appliance depreciation on its own, and you can run your property through the savings estimate.

What quietly kills it: a co-host, a cleaning service, or a property manager

The 100-hour test fails if any other individual spends more time on the activity than you; the substantially-all test fails if anyone else does meaningful work at all. A full-service manager who onboarded the listing, shot the photos and handled the first booking can easily out-hour an owner who closed three weeks ago.

So a co-host doing your setup counts against you, and so do cleaners — which is why a December turnover schedule is less dangerous than a December furnishing project someone else ran. Hours your spouse puts in are added to yours, one of the few places the rules help. And none of it works without records: not a reconstruction in April, but a dated log of what you did and how long it took, kept as you go. That is what makes a position audit-ready rather than merely arguable.

What to do in the next few weeks

Closing in the next month? List it and make it available before December 31, do the setup work yourself where you reasonably can, keep the log from day one, and order the study while the property is in its first depreciable year.

If your closing slips to January, nothing is lost — it becomes a 2027 deduction and you get a full year to clear material participation instead of three weeks. Sometimes that is the better trade.
