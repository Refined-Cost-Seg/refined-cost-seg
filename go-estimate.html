Bonus depreciation

For property acquired (contract signed) and placed in service after January 19, 2025, qualifying components may be eligible for 100% bonus depreciation. The estimate uses the rate that applies to you.

Questions we get

Will this create a problem if I'm audited?

A study should stand on its documentation. Ours shows the component detail and the cost basis behind every line, so the allocations can be traced rather than asserted. The method follows the IRS Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide, every study passes quality-control review before delivery, and Audit Support is available as an option.

Does my CPA have to be involved?

You don't need their permission to get an estimate. You will want them involved before you file: the study is a document they use, not a filing we make. Many clients loop their CPA in once they see the estimate, and we're happy to talk to them directly.

Do I need to have bought the property recently?

No. Studies are most often done in the year of purchase or after a renovation, but a property you've owned for several years can still be studied. If it was placed in service in a prior year, catching up the depreciation is handled by your CPA on Form 3115, and we'll explain what they need.

What if the numbers don't justify it?

Then we'll say so. The estimate exists so you can decide before spending anything. Smaller or lower-basis properties sometimes don't move enough to be worth the exercise, and we'd rather tell you that than sell you a study.
